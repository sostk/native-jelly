# C3 firmware policy and separate-output Opus experiments

30 October 2026. Application source: `cfe3b2ce782e4d1d2a37e90fd4ad724110066cbf` on `feat/opus-direct-play`, based on main `e673debb`.
The source tree was committed and clean before and after the gates and all device experiments.
This record and its companion documentation are a documentation-only successor.

## Scope

Native OPUS payload and raw packet feeding remain staged. Auto/Force routing and Jellyfin
DeviceProfiles still withhold Opus; no ordinary direct-play enablement is claimed. The new
firmware policy records exact build identity, reads decoder ranks conservatively, and applies
explicit hard audio restrictions in Force mode too. On `kisscurl-koli` the normal Opus candidate
is capped at two channels and Vorbis is excluded. A recognized audio capability table can
establish the staged pipeline-only Opus candidate even without an Opus file-player row.
Assumed/unmeasured capabilities do not establish that candidate.

A typed debug playurl can explicitly authorize 3–8 channel Opus only for its own URL, codec
and exact declared width. The shared header builder supplies family 0 for mono/stereo and
RFC 7845 family 1 Vorbis mappings for 3–8 channels. The selected container codec, width,
pre-skip, gain and mapping must match before either AU lane is fed. This experiment does
not modify normal capability limits, enable a software Opus decoder, or claim all legal headers.

## Physical TV results

Measured set: webOS 6.5.3, exact build 6.5.3-47, `kisscurl-koli`.
The firmware table lists six-channel Opus, while the decoder-rank file has no explicit
Opus rank rows. Absence of rank rows is not positive rank evidence.

Each fixture is synthetic H.264 1280×720 at 30 fps, 72 seconds, with identical copied video
and 48 kHz Opus. The surround sources use quiet independent noise, 20 ms packets; mono/stereo
use quiet tones. Small packets use CBR, large packets use VBR. These comparisons do not
isolate a precise byte threshold, bitrate versus VBR, or every layout/encoder combination.

TV speakers and HDMI ARC soundbar were selected and verified separately. The user could
not listen during the run. **These are pipeline/video-clock results, not audible output,
per-speaker channel separation, soundbar channel-count or subjective quality acceptance.**

| Fixture | Audio packets | TV speakers | HDMI ARC soundbar |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mono | family 0 | Played | Played |
| Stereo | family 0 | Played | Played |
| 5.1 small packets | 1200 bytes | Played | Played |
| 5.1 large packets | 2030–3890 bytes | Stalled | Stalled |
| 7.1 small packets | 1280 bytes | Stalled | Stalled |
| 7.1 large packets | 2069–4104 bytes | Stalled | Stalled |

Mono reached a sampled 32 seconds on each output; stereo reached 36 seconds and small-packet
5.1 reached 38 seconds. Stereo passed pause/resume and both native seeks; small-packet 5.1
passed both seeks. These cases used one Load with no reopen or watchdog reload.

Both large-packet 5.1 runs and all final 7.1 runs completed Load but failed to establish an
advancing playback clock. Captures show buffering/frozen pictures and filled queues. Small-packet
7.1 accepted both seek commands but did not play; a control-plane seek is not playback success.
No nonzero native type-18 errors or app crash signatures were seen in the completed fixtures.
The speaker negative test omitted the wide override and refused the surround declaration
before any OPUS Load. A separate normal-route stereo ceiling remains justified.

Two early 7.1 small-packet attempts stopped on an empty foreground-app ID, and an early
large-packet attempt was cancelled during batch cleanup. Those partial runs are not acceptance.
The user confirmed the TV was unused. The final driver distinguishes another named foreground
app (stop immediately) from an empty reply with an install-specific live process. Final repeats
completed their bounded windows and still stalled; no decoder success is inferred from liveness.

Service captures including the hardware video plane were inspected after releasing the TV.
Private raw logs, captures, fixtures and scripts remain outside Git. Sampled feed counters are
attempts and must not be presented as total accepted packets.

## Handback and output-setting limitations

Every bounded run closed the debug app, cleared automation, stopped its tunnel, restored the
panel when necessary, and released the TV lease. Final six soundbar runs confirm handback,
tunnel exit and restored output. The initial speaker mono run passed playback but its harness
restore assertion rejected a normalized route label; cleanup still closed the app and released
its lease, and read-only follow-up confirmed ARC restoration.

The initial service label was `external_arc_sound_mode_sync`. `setSoundOutput` accepted that
value but returned `external_arc`; the saved `soundModeSync` setting remained `on`. Tests
continued on the verified canonical ARC route. Do not claim the original special service label
was restored exactly. No volume or mute setter was invoked. This firmware's no-argument
`getVolume` response was not useful (unknown mute and implausible volume), so the wrapper's
numeric before/after sound summaries are not valid sound-setting verification.

## Remaining C3 work

1. Bounded persistent native-refusal evidence scoped to the exact firmware build, with
   preference compatibility and stale-record reconsideration.
2. One immutable effective-capability snapshot per play attempt, shared by local planning
   and Jellyfin DeviceProfile generation, leaving boot capabilities unchanged.
3. A single generation-bound recovery owner, integrated with existing rollback/HLS recovery:
   preserve audio selection, seek and pause intent; request audio conversion while copying
   video only where the existing route and Dolby contract allow it; cap retries.
4. Evidence classification: unsupported source headers are not demonstrated decoder failures.
   Current upstream pins named audio refusals/surround stalls directly; ambiguous 601 evidence
   requires two successful audio-conversion/video-copy/first-picture proofs without another Load.
   Port intentionally and test the distinction; never learn from cancellation or unrelated failures.
5. Negotiation, recovery, restart persistence, track changes and actual Jellyfin Opus acceptance,
   followed by listening when the user is available. Then enable eligible Auto/Force routes together.
