# v0.9.0 — More video formats, Russian, and playback fixes

<!-- Draft based on main at 133c1de1, compared with v0.8.0. Complete release verification before publishing. -->

Native Jelly can now ask Jellyfin to play AV1 and VP9 directly on televisions that advertise those decoders. This release also adds Russian and fixes playback problems involving Dolby Vision, DTS, converted audio and the end of a film.

## What's new

- **AV1 and VP9 direct play, including WebM.** Jellyfin can send these formats without converting the video when the television's decoder capabilities and the file allow it. Sets without a matching decoder still need server conversion; AV1 Dolby Vision is not offered.
- **Russian interface.** Choose Russian in **Settings → Language**, then restart the app, or let the app follow the television's language. Thanks to [ppichinenko](https://github.com/ppichinenko) for the translation.
- **Seeking without reloading the video on webOS 5 and newer.** Direct play now uses the television's native seek operation, intended to avoid a black screen and repeated HDR or Dolby Vision badges. If the television refuses it, playback falls back to reloading at the chosen position. This path still needs verification on a television.

## Fixed

- **Dolby Vision files containing HDR10+ could play as HDR10 or stall.** The app now recognises Jellyfin's Dolby Vision description for these files and removes the HDR10+ metadata that can stall the decoder while retaining static HDR10 metadata.
- **Dolby Vision could turn off when only the audio needed conversion.** Eligible HEVC video keeps its Dolby Vision declaration when Jellyfin copies it unchanged, including after an audio-track change. Re-encoding the video does not preserve Dolby Vision.
- **DTS tracks could be converted unnecessarily or fail to start.** Different names for the same codec now match correctly. **Force Direct Play** also stops offering DTS to televisions that do not advertise a DTS decoder.
- **Video could stutter when Jellyfin converted only the audio.** Copied video now keeps its original frame-rate declaration, including after an audio-track change.
- **A film could freeze on its last frame instead of finishing.** The player now recognises the television's end-of-stream signal even when the audio track extends beyond the last video frame.

## Compatibility

Playback and firmware checks for this release are pending. The previous release was watched on one 2021 LG C1 (OLED65C1PTB, webOS 6.5.3) using development builds; that does not verify this release's new playback paths. No wider playback claim is made here. Verification results belong in the [v0.9.0 technical audit](https://github.com/sostk/native-jelly/blob/main/docs/release-audits/v0.9.0.md) before publication.

## Known issues

- **Playback can fail over HTTPS through a reverse proxy.** This was reported on the maintainer's server before v0.8.0; these changes do not address it.
- **Video re-encoding remains H.264**, even when the server is allowed to encode HEVC.
- **AV1 playback has not been verified on a television for this release.** VP9 remains limited to 8-bit on webOS 5 and newer; the 10-bit and HDR10 path is limited to the older webOS 4 video path and also needs release-specific verification.

## Help test this release

On a television that advertises an AV1 or VP9 decoder, try a file in that format and check whether Jellyfin reports direct play. On webOS 5 or newer, try seeking in a directly played film, including HDR or Dolby Vision, and check whether the picture returns at the chosen position. [Report what happened](https://github.com/sostk/native-jelly/issues), with your TV model, firmware and whether playback was direct or converted. Reports of success are useful too.

## Installing

Download **com.sostk.nativejelly_0.9.0_arm.ipk** and install it with the [Homebrew Channel](https://github.com/webosbrew/webos-homebrew-channel) or [dev-manager-desktop](https://github.com/webosbrew/dev-manager-desktop). You do **not** need a rooted TV.

Nothing in this distribution chain is signed, so this sha256 is what tells you the file you have is the file published here:

```
__IPK_SHA256__  com.sostk.nativejelly_0.9.0_arm.ipk
```

[Installing and checking a download](https://github.com/sostk/native-jelly/blob/main/docs/install-and-verify.md) covers the other assets, how to check the hash on each platform, and the Developer Mode expiry that uninstalls your apps. This package bundles FFmpeg under LGPL-2.1-or-later and its complete corresponding source is attached to this release. Exactly what was built, verified and shipped is in the [technical audit for v0.9.0](https://github.com/sostk/native-jelly/blob/main/docs/release-audits/v0.9.0.md).
